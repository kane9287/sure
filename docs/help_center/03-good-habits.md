# Good Habits

A few habits that make this app pleasant to use instead of stressful. None of these are rules the app enforces — they're just what makes the experience better.

## If the ledger is off, don't dwell on it

Sooner or later, your balance in the app won't quite match your real bank balance — a few dollars here, a pending charge that settled differently than expected, a manual account that drifted out of sync. This is normal and it happens to everyone.

**If the ledger is off, feel free to reconcile the balance and move on. Don't dwell on the past.**

Here's how: open the account, use the **Update balance** action, and type in what your real-world balance actually is right now. The app automatically creates an adjustment entry behind the scenes to close the gap — you don't need to go hunting for the exact missing transaction that caused the drift. This adjustment only touches that account's balance; it doesn't get counted as spending in any budget category, so it won't throw off your Cap Mode caps or Envelope Mode assignments.

Chasing down a $3 mismatch from three weeks ago is almost never worth the time. Reconcile, move forward, and keep your energy for the transactions that actually matter.

## Categorize as you go, not in a big batch later

A transaction that's categorized right away (or auto-categorized by a Rule) is much less annoying than a pile of 40 Uncategorized transactions at the end of the month. If you're not sure what a transaction was, a rough guess is better than leaving it uncategorized — you can always fix it later.

## Set up Rules for repeat transactions

If you see yourself manually categorizing the same merchant over and over (coffee shops, your gym, your streaming subscriptions), turn it into a Rule. It's a five-second setup that saves you from doing the same click forever.

## Don't be afraid to try both budgeting modes

Cap Mode and Envelope Mode ([see the budgeting guide](02-budgeting.md)) suit different people. If one doesn't feel right after a couple of weeks, switch. Nothing is destroyed by switching — you're not locked in.

## Manual accounts need occasional attention

If an account isn't connected to a bank/provider, its balance only updates when you tell it to. That's fine, but it means it's on you to check in every so often (weekly or monthly, whatever fits) and reconcile it against your real statement — same "update balance and move on" approach as above.

## It's alpha software — expect rough edges

Things may look unfinished, break, or change between sessions. If something seems broken or confusing, that's useful feedback, not a sign you're doing something wrong. Say something rather than working around it quietly.
